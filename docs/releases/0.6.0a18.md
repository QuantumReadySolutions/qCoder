# qCoder 0.6.0a18

qCoder 0.6.0a18 is a behavior-changing pre-release successor to public qCoder 0.6.0a16. It is not
a stable or generally available release. Package publication does not activate a named-client
support claim.

This release preserves causal currentness for active-loop source, circuit, and result evidence.
Replacing an upstream artifact invalidates dependent downstream currentness without rewriting
immutable history. Current result registration requires strict manifests with sufficient circuit
lineage. Legacy bare counts fail closed, and unknown-lineage results remain historical and
non-current. Exact customer-selected paths remain structurally bounded without repository
discovery, and pending completion recovery does not authorize another execution.

Algorithm Intent clarification now returns one qCoder-supplied atomic continuation capsule. A
client copies the capsule unchanged and supplies only all advertised customer-reviewed correction
values plus the explicit review assertion. qCoder validates capsule integrity, embedded card and
revision identity, regenerated contract equality, required fields, types, domains, and content.
Malformed, tampered, stale, cross-card, cross-revision, incomplete, forbidden, and unauthorized
continuations fail closed with bounded diagnostics and no raw-value echo or retention.

Exactly twelve public Context Bridge tools and the two private coordination operations remain.
qCoder gains no authority to generate or edit customer source, execute a simulator, runtime,
hardware job, or next check, retain customer artifacts, or activate native-client authority.
Binding and adapter conformance do not by themselves establish qualification for any client,
client version, operating environment, topology, or workstyle.

This release makes no latency or quiet-operation guarantee, does not establish universal framework
neutrality, general framework qualification, or general PennyLane qualification, and does not
activate Tested, First-class, Client Compatibility, or other public support claims. Publication,
deployment, and qualification remain separate lifecycle decisions.
